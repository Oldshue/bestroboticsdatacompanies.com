How we researched and ranked robotics data companies

Last updated: 2026-11-09

What we did

  1. Built a candidate list. We searched current public sources for companies that collect, annotate or license robotics and physical-AI training data: teleoperation, egocentric video, UMI demonstrations, and LiDAR or sensor-fusion annotation.
  2. Checked each candidate on its own website. We read each company's official product pages on 2026-11-09 and recorded only what those pages state.
  3. Scored each company on five criteria:
    • Relevance: how directly the offering targets robotics and physical AI.
    • Coverage: collection modes and modalities (teleop, egocentric, UMI, depth, IMU, LiDAR, force/torque) and annotation depth.
    • Completeness: whether the company covers collection, QA, annotation, delivery and evals.
    • Transparency: public specs, formats, samples, provenance and pricing.
    • Evidence of operating at scale: named customers or partners and stated volumes, as claimed by the company.
  4. Ranked by overall fit for a typical robotics team that needs training data. Specialists for narrower needs, such as perception labeling or footage-to-episode conversion, are ranked lower but called out for the cases where they fit.

What we did not do

  • We did not test any product, run pilots, or train models on vendors' data.
  • We did not independently check volumes, sync accuracy, workforce sizes or customer results. Where we quote numbers, they are the company's own claims and are credited to the company.
  • We did not accept payment for placement. The list contains no affiliate links.

Limits

Most of our evidence comes from pages the vendors control. Several companies are young, and a live website shows that an offering is marketed, not that the company can deliver it at your volume. Ask for samples and run a pilot with written acceptance criteria.

Updates and corrections

We review the list when vendors change their offerings. If a company believes something here is out of date, the fix should cite a public source on its own site.
